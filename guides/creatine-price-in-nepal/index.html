Why creatine prices differ in Nepal

Three things change the price: the brand, the pack size and the seller. Imported brands like BPI Sports cost more after shipping and import duty, while Indian brands like MuscleBlaze and Wellcore cost less to bring into Nepal.

Flavour does not make creatine stronger. Flavoured tubs taste better in plain water, while unflavoured tubs mix into a shake without changing the taste. Micronised powder is ground finer, so it dissolves more easily, but the creatine itself is the same.

Be careful with prices that look too good. A well-known brand selling far below the usual creatine price in Nepal can mean an old, repacked or fake tub.

What creatine does

Your muscles use a fuel called phosphocreatine for short, hard efforts like a heavy set of squats or a sprint. Taking creatine every day tops up those stores. Research summarised by the International Society of Sports Nutrition shows creatine monohydrate can help you do a little more work in training, which over weeks supports strength and muscle gains. It is not a steroid and it is not a magic powder: it works only alongside regular training and enough protein.

How to take creatine

Daily dose: 3 to 5 g a day, every day, including rest days. This is the maintenance dose recommended in the International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand.

Loading or no loading? Both work.

  • With loading: about 0.3 g per kg of body weight per day for 5 to 7 days, split into 3 or 4 doses (about 18 g a day for a 60 kg person), then 3 to 5 g a day. Your muscles fill up in about a week.
  • Without loading: just take 3 to 5 g a day from day one. Your muscles fill up in about 3 to 4 weeks. This is simpler and easier on the stomach, so many beginners skip loading.

Timing: taking it every day matters far more than the exact time. Many people take it after training or with a meal because it is easy to remember.

Water: mix each scoop with a full glass of water, and drink normally through the day.

How to spot fake creatine in Nepal

Fake and refilled tubs are a real risk. Before you use any tub, check:

  • Seal: the inner foil seal must be unbroken when you open it.
  • Batch number and expiry date: printed clearly on the tub, not on a loose sticker.
  • Importer sticker: genuine imported tubs carry the importer's details.
  • QR or authenticity code: if the label has one, scan it and check it on the brand's official website.
  • Price: a tub far cheaper than the usual price in Nepal is a warning sign.
