  • Want protein with creatine already in it and train most days: BigMan Forza, Rs. 17,500.

Protein powder is a convenience, not a shortcut. The tub that helps most is the one you can afford to keep using and that fits your diet.

How much protein does a beginner need?

The International Society of Sports Nutrition suggests that most people who exercise do well on about 1.4 to 2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight per day, from food and shakes together (Jäger et al., 2017).

  • A 60 kg beginner: about 84 to 120 g a day.
  • A 70 kg beginner: about 98 to 140 g a day.
  • An 80 kg beginner: about 112 to 160 g a day.

Spread it over 3 or 4 meals. Use our free protein calculator for your own number, and see how much protein is in dal bhat and protein-rich vegetarian foods in Nepal to get more from food first.

How to take protein powder

  • Use the scoop in the tub and follow the serving size printed on the label.
  • Mix with water or milk. Milk adds more protein and calories; water keeps it lighter.
  • Most beginners only need one shake a day, after training or with a meal that is low in protein.
  • Count the shake as part of your daily protein, not on top of a diet that already has plenty.
  • On rest days, a shake is fine if your meals are short on protein.

If you have a kidney condition, a milk allergy or another health issue, ask your doctor before taking protein powder.
