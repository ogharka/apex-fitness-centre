You can eat at an Indian or Nepali restaurant and still lose weight. **The biggest calories usually come from the bread, the rice portion, the oil and the fried starters, not the curry itself.** Order one bread or one rice (not both), pick a protein curry, add a vegetable or dal, and skip the fried starters most of the time.

The numbers below come from USDA FoodData Central, as shown on our [food calorie chart](../../food/). Restaurant food often has more oil, butter and cream than these standard values, so treat them as the minimum.

Notice that one large naan has about the same calories as two curries. Bread and rice add up fast.

## The 5 rules for ordering

1. **One starch, not two.** Choose rice or a bread. If you share, share both.
2. **Pick a protein main.** Chicken, fish, mutton or paneer keep you full longer than a bread-heavy meal.
3. **Add a vegetable side or dal** instead of a second bread.
4. **Go easy on fried starters.** Samosa and pakora are fine now and then, but they rarely fill you up.
5. **Watch the extras.** Butter on the naan, ghee on the dal and sweet drinks add calories without adding fullness.

## Better orders, side by side

| Instead of | Try | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Butter naan and rice with your curry | One plain roti or a small rice | Saves a whole bread's worth of calories |
| Large biryani to yourself | Half a biryani and a vegetable side | Same taste, more fibre, smaller portion |
| Two fried starters | One starter shared, or a salad | Fried food adds a lot of oil |
| Creamy curry | A tomato-based curry or a tandoori dish | Less cream and butter, usually more protein for the calories |
| Sweet lassi or cola | Water, plain lassi or a small tea | Drinks add calories that do not fill you up |

## Three sample orders

**For weight loss:** chicken or fish curry, one roti, a side of dal or vegetables, water.

**For building muscle:** chicken biryani or a curry with rice, plus a dal or paneer side for extra protein.

**Sharing with family:** order one fewer bread than people at the table, add a vegetable dish and a dal, and let everyone serve themselves.

## Does one restaurant meal ruin a diet?

No. Weight change depends on what you eat over weeks, not one meal. If you eat out once or twice a week, enjoy it and follow the rules above. If you eat out most days, those rules matter much more. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the lowest-calorie Indian restaurant meal?
A tomato-based chicken or fish curry or a tandoori dish, with one plain roti and a vegetable side, is one of the lightest full meals on most menus.

### How many calories are in a naan?
About 262 kcal for a 90 g piece and about 378 kcal for a large 130 g restaurant naan, before any butter or garlic oil is added.

### Is biryani healthy for weight loss?
Biryani can fit, but portions are often large: a 500 g restaurant plate of chicken biryani is about 515 kcal. Share it, or eat half with a vegetable side.

### Is dal healthy?
Yes. Dal gives protein and fibre. A cup of dal made with oil is about 323 kcal and 16.5 g of protein, so watch the portion and extra ghee.

### Is roti better than naan?
For calories, yes: a medium roti is about 119 kcal, while a large naan is about 378 kcal.
