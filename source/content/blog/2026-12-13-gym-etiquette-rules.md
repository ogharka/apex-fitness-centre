Good gym etiquette is simple: **leave the equipment the way you would like to find it, share the space, and respect people's time and focus.** Nobody hands you these rules on the first day, so here they are.

## Equipment

### 1. Re-rack your weights

Put dumbbells back in the right place and strip plates off barbells and machines when you finish. Leaving 60 kg on the leg press for the next person is not a favour.

### 2. Wipe the bench and handles

Use the towel or spray provided after your set, especially if you sweat a lot. A small towel to lie on is even better.

### 3. Don't drop dumbbells and barbells

Lower weights with control. Dropping them damages equipment and floors, and startles people. (Heavy deadlifts in a dedicated area with proper plates are the exception if the gym allows it.)

### 4. Use collars (clips) on barbells

They stop plates from sliding off. Safer for you and everyone around you.

### 5. Return attachments

Cable handles, ropes and bars go back on their hooks.

## Sharing the space

### 6. Don't hog machines during busy hours

If you're doing several sets, let others "work in" between your sets. Avoid using one machine for 30 minutes at peak time.

### 7. Don't sit on equipment while scrolling

Resting between sets is fine. Long phone sessions on the bench while others wait is not.

### 8. Give people space

Don't stand right in front of the dumbbell rack or walk between a lifter and the mirror during their set.

### 9. Ask before taking equipment that seems in use

A towel or water bottle on a bench usually means someone is using it. "Are you using this?" solves most problems.

## Respect and safety

### 10. Don't give advice nobody asked for

Unless someone is about to get hurt, let the trainers coach. If you see real danger, tell a trainer.

### 11. Offer a spot only if you know how

If someone asks for a spot, ask how many reps they're going for and where they want help.

### 12. Keep your music to yourself

Use headphones. Not everyone shares your playlist.

### 13. Be careful with photos and videos

Filming your own sets is common, but make sure **other members aren't in the frame** without permission. Never film women or anyone else without consent.

## Hygiene

### 14. Come in clean clothes and indoor shoes

Fresh gym clothes and clean shoes keep the floor and equipment clean, especially in the monsoon.

### 15. Use deodorant, not a cloud of perfume

Strong body odour and strong perfume can both make training uncomfortable for others.

## For beginners: you belong here

Every regular at your gym was a beginner once. If you're unsure how something works, ask a trainer. Most people are happy to help when asked politely. Read our [gym guide for women](../gym-guide-for-women-first-visit/) and [how to choose a good gym](../how-to-choose-a-gym-in-nepal/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it rude to talk to people at the gym?

Not at all, but read the situation. If someone has headphones in and is mid-set, wait until they're resting.

### What does "working in" mean?

Sharing a machine or bench with someone by alternating sets while the other person rests.

### Should I bring my own towel?

Yes, if your gym doesn't provide one. A small towel for sweat and benches is good manners.

### Can I take photos in the gym?

Usually yes, of yourself, but avoid capturing other members without permission and follow your gym's rules.
