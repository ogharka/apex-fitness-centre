**Intermittent fasting can help you lose weight, but mainly because it helps you eat less, not because of a special fat-burning effect.** Studies comparing intermittent fasting with ordinary calorie reduction generally find **similar weight loss** when total calories are similar. If fasting makes it easier for you to eat less, it can be a useful tool. If it makes you overeat later, it is not for you.

## What is intermittent fasting?

Intermittent fasting means **eating within a set time window** and not eating outside it. Popular methods:

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| **16:8** | Eat within 8 hours (for example 10 AM to 6 PM), fast for 16 |
| **14:10** | A gentler version: eat within 10 hours |
| **5:2** | Eat normally 5 days, eat very little (around 500–600 kcal) 2 days a week |
| **Alternate-day fasting** | Very low intake every other day |

Many Nepalis already follow a pattern close to 14:10 naturally: morning tea, a late-morning khana, an afternoon snack and an evening meal.

## What does the research say?

A well-known trial published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* in 2022 compared **calorie restriction alone** with **calorie restriction plus time-restricted eating** (an 8-hour window) over 12 months in adults with obesity. Both groups lost weight, and the difference between them was **not statistically significant**. Other head-to-head studies have found similar results.

The takeaway: **the eating window itself is not magic. Eating less overall is what drives weight loss.**

## Possible benefits

- **Simple rule:** "no food after 7 PM" is easy to follow.
- **Less late-night snacking,** which is where many extra calories come from.
- **Some people feel more in control** of their eating.

## Possible downsides

- **Overeating** at the first meal after fasting.
- **Low energy** for early-morning training.
- **Less protein** if you squeeze meals into a short window.
- **Hard to keep up** with family meals, festivals and social life.

## Who should avoid intermittent fasting

- People with **diabetes**, especially on insulin or sugar-lowering medicines, unless supervised by a doctor
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding** women
- Anyone with a **history of eating disorders**
- **Under 18s**
- People with certain medical conditions or on medicines that need food

## How to try 14:10 or 16:8 with Nepali meals

| Time | Example (16:8, window 10 AM–6 PM) |
|---|---|
| Morning | Water, black tea or black coffee (no sugar or milk if strict) |
| 10:00 AM | Khana: rice, big bowl of dal, tarkari, eggs or chicken |
| 2:00 PM | Curd and fruit, or roasted chana |
| 5:30 PM | Roti, paneer or egg curry, vegetables |
| After 6 PM | Water, herbal tea |

**Keep protein high** and fill half your plate with vegetables. The plate method in our [weight loss diet plan with Nepali food](../weight-loss-diet-plan-nepali-food/) still applies.

## Intermittent fasting and the gym

- If you train **early morning**, a small snack before or a meal soon after may help performance and recovery.
- If you train **in the evening**, place your last meal after training within your window.
- **Total protein** across the day still matters for muscle. See [how much protein you need](../how-much-protein-do-you-need/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does intermittent fasting burn more fat?

Not significantly more than normal calorie reduction, according to most comparison studies. It works when it helps you eat less.

### Can I drink tea during fasting?

Black tea, black coffee and water are usually allowed. Milk tea with sugar breaks the fast.

### Will fasting make me lose muscle?

Not if you eat enough protein and keep strength training. Very aggressive fasting with low protein can cost muscle.

### Is 16:8 safe every day?

Many healthy adults do it without problems. If you feel dizzy, weak or start bingeing, stop and choose a different approach.
