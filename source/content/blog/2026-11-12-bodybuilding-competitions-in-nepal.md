Bodybuilding in Nepal has grown from a few dedicated lifters into a scene with **city and district championships, national events, and athletes winning medals at South Asian and Asian level**. If you're thinking of competing, the path usually starts with a **local or city show**, then national championships, and possibly selection for international events.

## Who runs bodybuilding in Nepal?

The national body is the **Nepal Bodybuilding and Fitness Federation (NBBFF)**. According to [The Himalayan Times](https://thehimalayantimes.com/sports/nbbff-to-host-asian-championship-next-month), the NBBFF hosted the **55th Asian Bodybuilding and Physique Sports Championship** in Kathmandu in 2023, the first time Nepal hosted that event, with 45 events across men's and women's categories.

Many cities and districts also hold their own championships, often with titles like **"Mr. Kathmandu"** or **"Mr. Dharan"**, organised by local associations and clubs.

## Recent international results

- At the **16th South Asian Bodybuilding and Physique Sports Championship** in Thimphu, Bhutan, in July 2026, Nepal's **Binu Gharti** won gold in women's swimwear and silver in model physique ([Hamro Patro](https://www.hamropatro.com/en/news/detail/774803045513786)).
- In 2016, **Nanita Maharjan** won bronze at the WBPF World Bodybuilding and Physique Sports Championships, reported as Nepal's first medal at that event.

## Common competition categories

Categories vary by event and federation, but common ones include:

| Category | What judges look for |
|---|---|
| **Bodybuilding** (by weight or height class) | Maximum muscle size, symmetry, definition |
| **Classic or athletic physique** | Muscle with a more classic, balanced look |
| **Men's physique** | Upper body shape, V-taper, presentation, usually in board shorts |
| **Women's model physique** | Toned, athletic shape and presentation |
| **Women's swimwear or bikini fitness** | Overall shape, conditioning and stage presence |
| **Masters and juniors** | Age-group categories |

Always read the **rules of the specific competition**, including height/weight classes and posing requirements.

## How to prepare for your first show

### 1. Build a base first

Most first-time competitors have at least **2 to 3 years of consistent training**. Pick a category that suits your physique.

### 2. Plan your prep timeline

Contest prep usually lasts **12 to 20 weeks** or more, depending on how lean you are at the start. Fat loss should be gradual to keep muscle.

### 3. Learn posing early

Posing can make a big difference. Practise mandatory poses in front of a mirror for months, not days. A coach or experienced competitor helps a lot.

### 4. Get the nutrition right

High protein, a controlled calorie deficit, and careful adjustments in the final weeks. Avoid extreme dehydration tricks, which are dangerous.

### 5. Stay away from dangerous drugs

Anabolic steroids and other drugs carry **serious health risks**, including heart, liver and hormonal problems, and are banned in tested competitions. A natural competition career is longer and safer.

### 6. Check health first

Get a medical check-up before starting a hard prep, especially if you are older or have health conditions.

## What a first show teaches you

Even if you don't win, your first competition teaches discipline, nutrition, posing and how your body responds to a diet. Many successful athletes placed outside the top 5 in their first shows.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I register for a bodybuilding competition in Nepal?

Follow local gyms, associations and the national federation on social media for event announcements. Registration details, categories and fees are published for each event.

### Do I need a coach to compete?

Not required, but a coach experienced in contest prep and posing makes the process safer and more effective.

### Is there a natural (drug-tested) bodybuilding category in Nepal?

Testing policies vary by event and federation. Ask the organiser before you register.

### How lean do you need to be to compete?

Very lean, usually much leaner than is comfortable for everyday life. This is why prep is temporary and needs careful planning.
