Working long shifts in Japan or Korea makes it hard to eat well, but a 90-minute cook once or twice a week solves most of it. **Cook a big pot of dal, a protein dish and rice on your day off, keep eggs and tofu in the fridge, and build every meal around a protein.** You can eat like home and still train hard.

This guide uses foods you can find in most Japanese and Korean supermarkets and Asian grocers. Nutrition numbers come from USDA FoodData Central, as shown on our [food calorie chart](../../food/).

## What to buy

| Protein | Why it works | Protein per serving |
|---|---|---|
| Eggs | Cheap, quick, easy to boil in advance | about 6.2 g per large egg |
| Tofu | Sold everywhere in Japan and Korea, no cooking needed | about 8.6 g per 120 g piece |
| Chicken breast or thigh | Cook a batch for 3 to 4 days | about 31 g per 100 g cooked breast |
| Lentils (masoor, mung) | From Asian or Indian grocers; makes dal | about 12.6 g per katori of dal |
| Soybeans or edamame | Frozen packs are easy to find | about 16 g per half cup cooked |
| Plain yoghurt | Like curd, good after shifts | about 5 g per katori |

Add rice, frozen vegetables, onions, garlic, ginger, tomatoes, and your spices from home.

## A 90-minute cooking plan

Do this on your day off:

1. **Rice:** cook a big pot and freeze portions in boxes.
2. **Dal:** one big pot of masoor or mung dal; keep 3 days in the fridge, freeze the rest.
3. **Protein curry:** a chicken, egg or soybean curry in a large pan.
4. **Vegetables:** a quick tarkari or stir-fry with frozen vegetables.
5. **Eggs:** boil 8 to 10 and keep them in the fridge for snacks and breakfasts.

Pack lunch boxes for the next 2 to 3 days straight away.

## Eating on long shifts

- **Pack protein snacks:** boiled eggs, tofu, yoghurt, a handful of nuts.
- **Eat before a long shift,** not only after. Low energy makes heavy work and training harder.
- **Convenience stores (konbini in Japan, pyeonuijeom in Korea)** sell boiled eggs, yoghurt and chicken products. Check labels for protein and watch fried foods and sweet drinks.
- **Drink water through the shift,** especially in summer or hot workplaces.

## How much protein do you need?

Most people who exercise do well on about 1.4 to 2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight a day ([ISSN](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/)). For a 65 kg worker, that is about 90 to 130 g a day. Check yours with the [protein calculator](../../calculators/protein-calculator/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the cheapest protein in Japan or Korea?
Eggs, tofu and dried lentils are usually among the cheapest protein foods. Prices change, so compare per gram of protein at your local shop.

### Can I make dal in Japan or Korea?
Yes. Lentils are sold in Asian and Indian grocers and online. Red lentils (masoor) cook fastest.

### How long does meal prep keep in the fridge?
Cooked dishes are usually best eaten within about 3 days. Freeze anything you will eat later.

### Is konbini food healthy?
Some is. Boiled eggs, yoghurt, tofu and salads are good choices; fried foods and sweet drinks are better as treats.

### How can I build muscle while working long shifts?
Eat protein at every meal, sleep as much as you can, and train 3 times a week with progressive weights or bodyweight exercises.
