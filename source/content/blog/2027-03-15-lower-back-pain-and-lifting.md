For most everyday lower back pain, staying active helps more than resting. **NHS guidance says people who keep moving recover more quickly, and that bed rest is no longer recommended** ([Dudley Group NHS](https://www.dgft.nhs.uk/pil/acute-low-back-pain/)). In the gym, that usually means lighter, pain-free training while your back settles, not stopping completely.

This guide is for common, non-serious back pain. If you have any of the warning signs listed at the end, see a doctor first.

## Why backs hurt in the gym

Most gym-related back pain is a strain: muscles and tissues around the spine that have been asked to do more than they were ready for. Common causes:

- **Doing too much too soon:** a big jump in weight or volume.
- Rounding under heavy load, especially in deadlifts and rows when tired.
- Ego reps: grinding out extra reps with poor form at the end of a set.
- Little sleep and lots of stress, which make pain feel worse.
- Long hours of sitting, then lifting without a warm-up.

Pain does not always mean damage. Backs are strong and most strains settle within days to weeks.

## Keep training: a simple plan

| Stage | What to do |
|---|---|
| First few days | Gentle walking every day. Light movements that do not increase pain. Skip heavy lifts. |
| When pain eases | Return to the gym. Train upper body and machines. Lower body with light weights and short range. |
| As you improve | Slowly bring back squats and deadlifts with lighter weights, building up over 2 to 4 weeks. |

A useful rule: **mild discomfort that settles within a day is fine; pain that gets worse or spreads down a leg is not.**

## Exercises that are usually easier on a sore back

- Walking, cycling on a stationary bike
- Machine chest press, lat pulldown, seated cable row (with a supported chest if needed)
- Leg press with a short range, if pain-free
- Glute bridges
- Bird-dog and dead bug for core control
- Side planks, short holds

## Exercises to modify for a while

| Exercise | Change |
|---|---|
| Deadlift | Lighter weight, or a trap-bar or Romanian deadlift with a small range |
| Barbell squat | Goblet squat to a box at a comfortable depth |
| Bent-over row | Chest-supported row or seated cable row |
| Sit-ups | Swap for dead bugs and planks |
| Heavy overhead press | Seated press with lighter dumbbells |

## Prevent the next flare-up

1. **Warm up** before heavy sets. See [warm-up before the gym](../warm-up-before-gym/).
2. Learn to brace: take a big breath into your stomach and tighten your core before each heavy rep. A belt helps on heavy sets but does not prevent injury on its own; see [do you need a lifting belt?](../do-you-need-a-lifting-belt/).
3. Add weight slowly: small jumps every week. See [progressive overload explained](../progressive-overload-explained/).
4. Practise technique with lighter weights: [how to deadlift for beginners](../how-to-deadlift-for-beginners/) and [how to squat properly](../how-to-squat-properly/).
5. Move during long sitting days: stand and walk a few minutes every hour. Our [stretches for desk workers](../stretching-for-desk-workers/) help too.

## When to see a doctor or physiotherapist

Get **urgent** medical help if back pain comes with any of these ([Dudley Group NHS](https://www.dgft.nhs.uk/pil/acute-low-back-pain/)):

- Numbness around your bottom or genitals
- Loss of control of your bladder or bowels, or leaking urine or stool
- Changes in sensation in your genitals

Also see a doctor if:

- The pain is severe and has not improved after about six weeks
- You have numbness, tingling or weakness in a leg
- The pain started after a fall or accident
- You also have fever, unexplained weight loss, or you feel unwell

A physiotherapist can find the cause of ongoing pain and give you a plan.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I stop going to the gym with lower back pain?
Usually not completely. Keep moving with gentle, pain-free exercise and lighter weights, and avoid the lifts that make it worse until it settles.

### Is walking good for lower back pain?
Yes. Gentle walking is one of the best ways to stay active while your back recovers.

### Can deadlifts cause back pain?
Any heavy lift can strain your back if the weight jumps too fast or form breaks down. Done well and built up slowly, deadlifts strengthen the back.

### Does a lifting belt prevent back pain?
Research does not show belts reliably prevent back injuries. Good technique and sensible progress matter more.

### How long does gym back pain last?
Many simple strains feel much better within days to a few weeks. See a doctor if it is severe or not improving after about six weeks.
