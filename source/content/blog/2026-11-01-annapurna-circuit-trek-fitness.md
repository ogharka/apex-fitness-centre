The Annapurna Circuit is one of the world's great treks, and you do **not** need to be an athlete to complete it. You need the stamina to **walk 5 to 7 hours a day for around two weeks**, and the strength and patience for one big day: crossing **Thorong La Pass at about 5,416 m**.

Train your legs and lungs for **8 to 10 weeks**, plan your acclimatisation sensibly, and the circuit is achievable for most healthy adults.

## How hard is the Annapurna Circuit?

- **Length:** the full circuit is often quoted at around 160 to 230 km, depending on where you start and finish and how much of the road sections you skip by jeep.
- **Duration:** usually **12 to 18 days** of trekking.
- **Highest point:** Thorong La, about **5,416 m**.
- **Pass day:** often the longest and toughest day, with an early start, a steep climb from Thorong Phedi or High Camp, and a long, knee-testing descent of over 1,500 m to Muktinath.
- **Daily walking:** typically 5 to 7 hours.

The trail itself is not technical. The challenge is **altitude and consecutive days of walking**.

## An 8 to 10 week training plan

| Weeks | Main training | Extras |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | 3 brisk walks of 45–60 min, 1 hilly walk of 2 hours | Strength 2x a week |
| 4–6 | Weekend hike of 3–5 hours with a 6–8 kg pack; stairs or incline 2x a week | Strength 2x a week |
| 7–9 | Back-to-back weekend hikes (Sat + Sun); one long day of 6+ hours with lots of downhill | Strength 2x a week |
| 10 | Light walking, stretching, rest before the trek | — |

**Key strength exercises**, 3 sets of 10 to 15, twice a week:

- Step-ups with a backpack
- Walking lunges and reverse lunges
- Squats (see our [squat guide](../how-to-squat-properly/))
- Slow step-downs from a box for downhill strength
- Calf raises
- Plank and side plank

## Train for the downhill

Most trekkers prepare for climbing and are surprised by how much the **descents** hurt, especially from Thorong La to Muktinath. Prepare by:

- **Walking down hills and stairs slowly** in training.
- **Doing slow step-downs** and lunges.
- **Using trekking poles**, which take a lot of strain off the knees.

## Acclimatisation matters more than fitness

Fitness makes the trek more enjoyable, but it does not protect you from altitude sickness.

- Follow an itinerary with **acclimatisation days**, commonly at **Manang**.
- **Go slow** above 3,000 m, and do short higher walks in the afternoon.
- Drink water, avoid alcohol, and eat well.
- **Watch for symptoms:** headache, nausea, dizziness, poor sleep. Severe symptoms such as confusion, loss of balance or breathlessness at rest mean **descend immediately**.

Talk to a doctor before the trip about altitude and any health conditions.

## Practical tips

- **Permits:** check the latest requirements for the Annapurna Conservation Area before you go.
- **Boots:** break them in with at least 3 to 4 long hikes.
- **Layers:** mornings near the pass can be well below freezing.
- **Start early on pass day,** and do not be afraid to turn back in bad weather.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Annapurna Circuit harder than Everest Base Camp?

Both are similar in daily effort. The circuit is longer and has the high Thorong La crossing; EBC spends more consecutive days at very high altitude. Preparation is very similar.

### Can beginners do the Annapurna Circuit?

Yes, with 2 to 3 months of training and a sensible itinerary with acclimatisation days.

### How many hours a day do you walk?

Usually 5 to 7 hours, with a longer, harder day for the pass.

### What is the hardest part of the Annapurna Circuit?

For most trekkers, the Thorong La crossing day: an early, cold, high-altitude climb followed by a long descent.
