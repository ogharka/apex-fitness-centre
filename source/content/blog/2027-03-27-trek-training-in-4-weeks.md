Four weeks is not much, but it is enough to make your trek noticeably easier. **You cannot build big fitness in a month, but you can get your legs used to long walks and stairs, break in your boots, and learn your pace.** Pair that with a slow, sensible itinerary and you will enjoy the trek far more than if you did nothing.

## What you can and cannot change in 4 weeks

| You CAN | You CANNOT |
|---|---|
| Get your legs used to hours of walking | Become very fit from a low starting point |
| Toughen your feet and break in your boots | Train for altitude at home |
| Strengthen your legs for descents a little | Make up for an itinerary that climbs too fast |
| Learn your comfortable pace | Undo years of no exercise |

That last column is why your trek choice matters. If you are starting from little exercise, choose a shorter, lower trek; see our guide to [short treks in Nepal](../short-treks-in-nepal-fitness-guide/).

## The 4-week plan

Train 5 days a week. Keep every session at a pace where you can still talk.

### Week 1: Build the habit
- 3 walks of 45 to 60 minutes, including any hills or stairs you can find
- 2 strength sessions (below)
- Weekend: a 2.5 to 3 hour walk in the boots you will trek in

### Week 2: Add climbing
- 2 stair or incline sessions of 40 minutes
- 1 walk of 60 minutes
- 2 strength sessions
- Weekend: a 3.5 to 4 hour walk with a 4 to 5 kg backpack

### Week 3: Go long (your hardest week)
- 2 stair or incline sessions of 45 to 50 minutes
- 2 strength sessions
- Weekend: a 5 hour walk with your trek pack, then 2 to 3 hours the next day

### Week 4: Taper
- 2 easy walks of 45 minutes
- 1 light strength session
- Rest 2 to 3 days before you travel

Do not try to make up for lost time with very hard training in the last week. Arrive rested.

## The strength session

Twice a week, 25 to 30 minutes:

1. Step-ups with a slow step down, 3 x 10 each leg
2. Bodyweight or goblet squats, 3 x 12
3. Reverse lunges, 2 x 10 each leg
4. Calf raises on a step, 3 x 15
5. Plank, 3 x 30 seconds

The slow step down matters most: it trains the muscles that brake on long descents. More tips in [7 ways to stop knee pain when hiking downhill](../knee-pain-hiking-downhill/).

## Make the trek itself easier

- **Hire a porter** and carry only a day pack. See [how heavy should your trekking backpack be](../how-heavy-should-trekking-backpack-be/).
- Use two trekking poles, especially going down.
- Walk slowly from the first day. Fast starts cause most bad days.
- Choose an itinerary with rest days, and do not skip them.
- Eat and drink well: [what to eat on a Nepal trek](../what-to-eat-on-nepal-trek/).

## Altitude: the part training cannot fix

Even fit trekkers get altitude sickness if they go up too fast; fitness does not protect you ([TravelHealthPro](https://travelhealthpro.org.uk/factsheets/altitude-illness)). Above 3,000 m, sleep no more than about 500 m higher each night and take a rest day every three or four days. Read [altitude sickness on Nepal treks](../altitude-sickness-nepal-trek/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 4 weeks enough to train for a trek?
It is enough to make a noticeable difference, especially if you are already fairly active. Pair it with a slow itinerary and a light pack.

### Can I do Everest Base Camp with 4 weeks of training?
Many active people do, but it is a long, high trek. If you are starting from very little exercise, consider a shorter trek or more training time.

### What is the best exercise to train for trekking?
Long walks with your backpack, stair climbing, and step-ups with a slow step down.

### Should I train hard in the last week?
No. Keep it easy and arrive rested. Hard training right before the trek leaves you tired.

### How do I break in trekking boots quickly?
Wear them on every training walk and around the house from day one, with the socks you will trek in.
