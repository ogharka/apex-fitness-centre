Home-cooked Nepali and Indian food is hard to count because one pot feeds the whole family and nobody measures the oil. **The easy method: count the oil and ghee that go into the pot, weigh your rice and dal once or twice to learn your portions, and use standard values for the rest.** You do not need to weigh every meal forever. A few weeks of counting teaches you what a normal portion looks like.

## Why home food is tricky

- **One pot, many plates.** A curry for four people is hard to split into calories per person.
- **Oil and ghee are invisible.** They are the most energy-dense part of the meal and the easiest to forget: one tablespoon of mustard oil is about 124 kcal ([mustard oil calories](../../food/mustard-oil-calories/)), and one tablespoon of ghee about 126 kcal ([ghee calories](../../food/ghee-calories/)).
- **Portions vary.** One person's "one plate of rice" can be double another's.

## Step 1: Count the oil

Oil is where most counting mistakes happen. When you cook:

1. Measure the oil or ghee with a spoon before it goes in the pan.
2. Write down the total for the pot.
3. Divide by the number of people eating.

**Example:** 3 tablespoons of mustard oil in a family curry for 4 people is about 372 kcal in the pot, or about 93 kcal each from oil alone.

## Step 2: Weigh your rice and dal once

Use a cheap kitchen scale for one or two meals:

- Put your empty plate on the scale and press zero.
- Serve your normal rice and weigh it.
- Do the same with your bowl of dal.

Now you know your real portion. A Nepali plate of rice is often around 300 g cooked, which is about 390 kcal ([white rice calories](../../food/rice-white-calories/)). A katori of dal (150 g) made with oil is about 248 kcal ([dal calories](../../food/dal-calories/)).

## A worked example: one dinner

| Item | Amount | Calories |
|---|---|---|
| Rice | 250 g | about 325 kcal |
| Dal (made with oil) | 1 katori (150 g) | about 248 kcal |
| Saag | 1 cup | about 36 kcal |
| Chicken curry | 1 cup | about 194 kcal |
| Extra ghee on the rice | 1 teaspoon | about 45 kcal |
| **Total** | | **about 850 kcal** |

Swap 100 g of rice for a second cup of saag and the same dinner drops by about 90 kcal, with more fibre.

## Easy shortcuts

- **Plate method instead of counting:** half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter rice. Our [Nepali diet plan for weight loss](../weight-loss-diet-plan-nepali-food/) uses it.
- **Use the same bowls every day.** Once you know your katori holds about 150 g, you never have to weigh dal again.
- **Count drinks.** Three cups of sweet milk tea a day is over 350 kcal.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I count calories in homemade curry?
Add up the main ingredients for the whole pot (especially the oil, meat and any cream), then divide by the number of portions. Or use a standard value, like 194 kcal for a cup of chicken curry, and add the oil you know you used.

### How many calories are in a katori of dal?
About 248 kcal for a 150 g katori of dal made with oil, with about 12.6 g of protein. Thin, watery dal has less.

### Do I need a food scale?
A cheap kitchen scale helps for the first week or two. After that, most people can judge portions by eye using the same plates and bowls.

### Should I count the oil used in cooking?
Yes. Oil is the most energy-dense part of home cooking, and forgetting it is the most common reason counting does not match results.

### Is counting calories necessary to lose weight?
No. Many people do well with the plate method. Counting for a few weeks simply teaches you what your portions really are.
