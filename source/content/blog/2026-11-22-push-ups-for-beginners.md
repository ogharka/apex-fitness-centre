If you cannot do a single push-up today, you can almost certainly do **10 to 20 good ones within 6 to 8 weeks**. The secret is to start with an easier version you *can* do for clean reps, and step up only when it becomes easy.

Push-ups train your **chest, shoulders, triceps and core** at the same time, need no equipment, and you can do them anywhere.

## Perfect push-up form

1. **Hands** slightly wider than your shoulders, fingers pointing forward.
2. **Body in a straight line** from head to heels. Squeeze your glutes and tighten your stomach.
3. **Lower your chest** towards the floor, elbows about **45 degrees** from your body, not flared straight out.
4. **Go down until your chest is a fist's height from the floor.**
5. **Push the floor away** and straighten your arms fully at the top.

Breathe in on the way down, out on the way up.

## The progression ladder

Start at the level where you can do **3 sets of 8 to 12 clean reps**. When you can do **3 sets of 15**, move up a level.

| Level | Exercise | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wall push-up**: hands on a wall, feet a step back | Very light, learns the pattern |
| 2 | **Table or bed push-up**: hands on a sturdy table edge | More body weight |
| 3 | **Step or stair push-up**: hands on a low step | Close to the floor version |
| 4 | **Knee push-up**: knees on the floor, straight line from knees to head | Good bridge to full push-ups |
| 5 | **Full push-up** | The goal |
| 6 | **Feet-raised push-up** or **slow 3-second lowering** | Harder versions |

**Negatives** also help: start at the top of a full push-up and lower yourself as slowly as you can (3 to 5 seconds), then reset on your knees. Do 3 sets of 5.

## A 6-week plan

Train **3 days a week** (for example Sunday, Tuesday, Thursday).

| Week | Plan |
|---|---|
| 1 | 3 sets of your current level, 8–12 reps |
| 2 | 3 sets, add 2 reps per set |
| 3 | Move up one level if you hit 3 x 15; add 3 negatives |
| 4 | 4 sets at your new level |
| 5 | Move up again; try 1–3 full push-ups at the start of the session |
| 6 | Test: as many full push-ups as possible with good form |

Write your reps down each session. Small weekly improvements add up quickly.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Hips sagging | Squeeze glutes and stomach, or go back a level |
| Bottom sticking up in the air | Imagine a straight plank from head to heels |
| Elbows flared out wide | Tuck elbows to about 45° |
| Half reps | Lower until the chest is close to the floor |
| Head dropping | Look at the floor slightly in front of your hands |

## Push-ups for women

Women often start with fewer push-ups because of less upper-body muscle, not because push-ups are "not for women." The same progression works perfectly. Many women reach full push-ups within a couple of months.

## Make push-ups part of a full routine

Push-ups are a great upper-body exercise, but balance them with **pulling exercises** (rows, band pull-aparts, or pull-up progressions) and **leg work**. See our [home workout without equipment](../home-workout-without-equipment/) for a full plan.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many push-ups should a beginner do?

Start with a version you can do for 8 to 12 clean reps, 3 sets, 3 times a week. Quality matters more than numbers.

### Are knee push-ups useful?

Yes. They build the same muscles with less weight and are a great step towards full push-ups.

### Can push-ups build chest muscle?

Yes, especially for beginners. As you get stronger, make them harder or add weighted exercises at the gym.

### Should I do push-ups every day?

Not as a beginner. Rest a day between sessions so your muscles can recover and get stronger.
