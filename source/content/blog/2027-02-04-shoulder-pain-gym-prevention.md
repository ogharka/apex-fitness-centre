Shoulder pain is one of the most common gym complaints. The shoulder is a very mobile joint, which makes it flexible but also easy to overload. Most gym-related shoulder pain comes from **too much pressing, poor technique, too little upper-back work, and jumping weights too fast**. Fortunately, most of it is preventable.

*This guide is about prevention. If you already have pain, especially sharp or persistent pain, see a doctor or physiotherapist.*

## Common causes in the gym

1. **Too much pressing, not enough pulling:** lots of bench press and shoulder press, few rows and pulldowns.
2. **Elbows flared wide on bench press:** puts the shoulder in a vulnerable position. See [how to bench press safely](../how-to-bench-press-safely/).
3. **Behind-the-neck presses and pulldowns** for people without the mobility for them.
4. **Ego lifting:** heavy weights with poor control.
5. **Skipping warm-ups,** especially on cold mornings.
6. **Poor posture** from long hours of sitting.

## Prevention: 6 habits

### 1. Balance push and pull

For every pressing set, do at least one pulling set. Many lifters do even more pulling: rows, pulldowns, face pulls.

### 2. Warm up the shoulders

Before upper-body training: arm circles, band pull-aparts, light face pulls and a few light sets of your first press. See [the 10-minute warm-up](../warm-up-before-gym/).

### 3. Press with good technique

- Shoulder blades **back and down** on bench press
- Elbows about **45 to 70 degrees** from the body
- **Controlled lowering**
- On overhead press, press the bar **in front of your face**, not behind the neck

### 4. Strengthen the upper back and rotator cuff

Add 2 to 3 sets of these, twice a week:

- **Face pulls** with a rope
- **Band pull-aparts**
- **External rotations** with a light cable or band
- **Rows** of all kinds

### 5. Progress gradually

Increase weights in small steps. Read [progressive overload explained](../progressive-overload-explained/).

### 6. Work on posture and mobility

Desk workers especially benefit from upper-back mobility and chest stretches. See [stretching routine for desk workers](../stretching-for-desk-workers/).

## Exercises to modify if they hurt

| Painful exercise | Try instead |
|---|---|
| Barbell bench press | Dumbbell press or neutral-grip (palms facing) press |
| Behind-the-neck press | Front press or landmine press |
| Dips | Close-grip push-ups or pushdowns |
| Upright rows | Lateral raises with lighter weight |

## When to see a professional

- Pain that is **sharp, lasts more than 1 to 2 weeks**, or gets worse
- **Night pain** or pain at rest
- **Weakness**, clicking with pain, or loss of movement
- Pain after a **fall or sudden injury**
- **Numbness or tingling** in the arm

## A 5-minute shoulder warm-up

Do this before any upper-body session:

1. **Arm circles:** 10 forwards, 10 backwards
2. **Band pull-aparts:** 2 × 15
3. **Band external rotations:** 2 × 12 each arm, elbow at your side
4. **Wall slides:** 2 × 10, keeping your lower back against the wall
5. **Light warm-up sets:** 1–2 sets of your first pressing exercise with an empty bar or light dumbbells

## A simple push-to-pull balance

For every pressing exercise, include at least one pulling exercise. For example:

| Push | Pull |
|---|---|
| Bench press | Seated cable row |
| Overhead press | Lat pulldown or pull-ups |
| Push-ups | Dumbbell rows |
| Dips | Face pulls |

Many lifters do extra pulling (rows and face pulls) to balance years of sitting and pressing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I train through shoulder pain?

No. Mild discomfort that disappears with warm-up may be okay, but sharp or worsening pain means stop and get it checked.

### Are shoulder presses bad for your shoulders?

Not for most people with good technique and sensible weights. Pressing in front of the head is usually safer than behind the neck.

### What's the best exercise for shoulder health?

Face pulls, rows and band pull-aparts are excellent for balance and upper-back strength.

### Can a lifting belt protect my shoulders?

No. A belt helps your core brace on heavy lifts; it doesn't protect the shoulders.
