Omega-3 fats are important for your **heart, brain and overall health**. The best way to get them is from **oily fish about twice a week**. If you rarely eat fish, a fish oil supplement is a reasonable option, but it's a **health supplement, not a muscle builder**. Don't expect bigger lifts from it.

## What are omega-3s?

There are three main types:

- **EPA and DHA:** found mainly in fish and seafood; the forms your body uses directly.
- **ALA:** found in plant foods such as flaxseed (aalas), walnuts, chia and mustard oil. Your body converts only a small amount of ALA into EPA and DHA.

## What the evidence suggests

- **Heart health:** eating fish regularly is linked with better heart health. Supplement results are more mixed, with benefits clearer for some people with existing heart conditions or high triglycerides, under medical advice.
- **Triglycerides:** high doses of omega-3 can lower blood triglycerides, used medically under supervision.
- **Joints and soreness:** some studies suggest small reductions in joint stiffness or muscle soreness, but effects are modest.
- **Muscle growth:** no strong evidence that fish oil builds muscle in people who train and eat enough protein.

## Who might benefit from a supplement

- People who **rarely or never eat fish**
- Some people with **high triglycerides** (with medical advice)
- People who want a simple daily habit for general health

## How to choose a fish oil supplement

1. **Check EPA + DHA per serving**, not just "1000 mg fish oil." The total fish oil amount is not the same as omega-3 content.
2. **Buy genuine products** from sellers who can show import details. See [how to spot fake supplements in Nepal](../fake-supplements-in-nepal-how-to-spot-them/).
3. **Check the expiry date** and store away from heat and sunlight.
4. **Fishy burps?** Take capsules with a meal.

## Safety

At normal label doses, fish oil is generally well tolerated. Possible side effects include fishy taste, burping or stomach upset. People taking **blood-thinning medicines** or preparing for surgery should talk to a doctor first, because high doses can affect bleeding.

## How to read a fish oil label

The big number on the front (usually "1000 mg") is the total **fish oil**, not the omega-3s. What matters is **EPA + DHA**, listed on the back.

| Label says | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Fish oil 1000 mg | Total oil in the capsule |
| EPA 180 mg + DHA 120 mg | The actual omega-3s: **300 mg** per capsule |
| "Triple strength" | Often 600–900 mg EPA + DHA per capsule |

**Example:** if your capsule has 300 mg of EPA + DHA and you're aiming for about 500 mg a day (a common general target), you'd need about 2 capsules a day. Higher doses for medical reasons should come from your doctor.

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing by price per capsule** instead of price per mg of EPA + DHA.
- **Buying very cheap, unknown brands.** Fish oil can go rancid. A strong fishy smell or taste when you open the bottle is a bad sign.
- **Expecting it to build muscle.** It supports general health; training and protein build muscle.
- **Storing it in a hot place.** Keep it cool and away from sunlight, especially in the Terai summer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does fish oil help build muscle?

Not meaningfully for most people. It's a health supplement rather than a muscle-building one.

### Can vegetarians get omega-3?

Yes, ALA from flaxseed, walnuts, mustard oil and soybean. Algae-based omega-3 supplements provide EPA and DHA without fish.

### When should I take fish oil?

With a meal, which improves absorption and reduces fishy burps.

### Is fish oil safe every day?

At normal doses, generally yes for healthy adults. Ask your doctor if you take medicines, especially blood thinners.

### How do I know if my fish oil has gone off?

Break a capsule open. Fresh fish oil smells mildly of the sea; rancid oil smells strongly fishy or like old paint. Throw it out if so.
