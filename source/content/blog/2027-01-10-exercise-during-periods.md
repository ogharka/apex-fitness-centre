**Yes, for most women it's safe to exercise during your period**, and many find that light to moderate exercise **eases cramps, lifts mood and reduces bloating**. There's no medical reason to stop training just because you're menstruating. The key is to **listen to your body**: train normally on good days and go lighter on difficult ones.

In Nepal, some families still have strong traditions around menstruation. Physically, exercising during your period is a normal, healthy choice.

## How exercise can help

- **Cramps:** movement increases blood flow and releases endorphins, which may ease pain for some women.
- **Mood:** exercise can reduce irritability and low mood.
- **Bloating:** gentle movement and walking can help.
- **Energy:** many women feel more energetic after a light session than after resting all day.

## What to do on different days

| How you feel | Try |
|---|---|
| **Good energy** | Your normal workout |
| **Mild cramps or tiredness** | Lighter weights, fewer sets, walking, cycling |
| **Heavy flow, strong cramps** | Gentle walk, stretching, yoga, or rest |
| **Very heavy bleeding, dizziness, severe pain** | Rest, and see a doctor if it's unusual for you |

## Practical tips

1. **Use the protection you're most comfortable with** for movement: pads, tampons, menstrual cups or period underwear.
2. **Wear dark, comfortable clothing** if it makes you feel more confident.
3. **Drink enough water.** See [how much water you should drink](../how-much-water-should-you-drink/).
4. **Eat iron-rich foods** if you have heavy periods: leafy greens (saag), lentils, eggs, meat.
5. **Warm up longer** if you feel stiff.
6. **Skip very intense sessions** on your worst day if they make you feel unwell.

## Training across your cycle

Some women notice changes in energy and strength across their cycle. Common patterns:

- **First half of the cycle (after your period):** many feel stronger and more energetic, a good time to push.
- **Second half (before your period):** some feel more tired or bloated; lighter training may feel better.

These patterns vary a lot between women. Tracking how you feel in a notes app for 2 or 3 months shows you **your** pattern.

## When to see a doctor

- Periods that are **very heavy**, very painful, or stop you from normal activities
- **Missing periods** for several months, especially with intense training or dieting
- **Dizziness, fainting** or signs of anaemia (unusual tiredness, breathlessness)

Missing periods because of very low body weight or extreme training is a sign your body isn't getting enough energy. That needs medical attention.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it bad to lift weights during your period?

No. If you feel well, lifting is safe. Go lighter if you feel tired or crampy.

### Can exercise make period cramps worse?

Some women find very intense exercise uncomfortable on heavy days. Gentle exercise more often helps.

### Should I skip the gym on my period?

Only if you feel unwell. Many women train as normal.

### Can exercise change my cycle?

Moderate exercise usually doesn't. Very high training loads with low food intake can disrupt periods; if that happens, see a doctor.
