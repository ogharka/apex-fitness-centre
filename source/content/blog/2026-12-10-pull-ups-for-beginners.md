The pull-up is one of the hardest bodyweight exercises, and one of the most satisfying to achieve. Most beginners can get their **first full pull-up in 6 to 12 weeks** with the right progression, even if they cannot hang from the bar for 10 seconds today.

Pull-ups build your **back (lats), biceps, forearms and grip**, and give you that wide, V-shaped upper body.

## Correct pull-up form

1. **Grip** the bar slightly wider than shoulder width, palms facing away.
2. **Hang** with straight arms, shoulders slightly pulled down (not shrugged to your ears).
3. **Pull** your chest towards the bar, driving your elbows down towards your back pockets.
4. **Chin over the bar** at the top.
5. **Lower slowly** to straight arms. No swinging or kicking.

**Chin-ups** (palms facing you) are usually a little easier because the biceps help more. Many people get a chin-up first.

## The 5-step progression

### Step 1: Dead hang

Hang from the bar with straight arms. Build to **3 sets of 30 seconds**. This builds grip and gets your shoulders used to hanging.

### Step 2: Rows

- **Inverted rows** under a Smith machine bar or a low, sturdy bar, body straight, pulling your chest to the bar.
- **Lat pulldowns** and **seated cable rows** on machines.

Aim for **3 sets of 10 to 12**, twice a week.

### Step 3: Scapular pulls

Hang from the bar and, without bending your arms, pull your shoulder blades **down and together** so your body rises a few centimetres. **3 sets of 8.** This teaches you to start the pull with your back.

### Step 4: Negatives

Jump or step up so your chin is over the bar, then **lower yourself as slowly as possible**, aiming for 3 to 5 seconds. **3 sets of 3 to 5.** This is one of the most effective steps.

### Step 5: Assisted pull-ups

Use a **resistance band** looped over the bar under your knee or foot, or an **assisted pull-up machine**. Reduce the help over time.

## An 8-week plan (2 sessions a week)

| Weeks | Main work |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Dead hangs, lat pulldowns, inverted rows |
| 3–4 | Add scapular pulls and negatives (3 x 3) |
| 5–6 | Negatives (3 x 5), band-assisted pull-ups (3 x 5) |
| 7–8 | Lighter band, try 1 full pull-up at the start of each session |

## Tips that speed things up

- **Lose a little body fat** if you have some to lose. Less weight to pull makes a big difference.
- **Train your grip** with dead hangs and farmer's walks.
- **Never swing** to get your chin over the bar. It doesn't build strength.
- **Rest 2 to 3 minutes** between hard pull-up sets.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Half reps | Full straight-arm hang at the bottom |
| Kipping and swinging | Slow, controlled reps, legs still |
| Shrugging shoulders | Pull shoulders down first |
| Only doing pulldowns | Include negatives and hangs |

## Frequently asked questions

### Why can't I do a single pull-up?

Most beginners lack back and grip strength, or carry extra body weight. The progression above builds both.

### Are pull-ups good for women?

Yes. Women can absolutely learn pull-ups. It may take a little longer, and assisted versions are a great start.

### How many pull-ups should I be able to do?

There is no single standard. Being able to do 5 to 10 clean pull-ups is a great fitness goal for most people.

### Can I do pull-ups at home?

Yes, with a sturdy doorway bar fixed properly. Make sure it is rated for your weight.
