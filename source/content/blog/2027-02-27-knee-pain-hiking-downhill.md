Knees often hurt more going down a mountain than going up. **On a descent, your thigh muscles work as brakes with every step, and much of the load goes through the front of the knee.** The good news is that most downhill knee pain can be reduced with poles, a slower pace, stronger legs and a lighter pack.

Treks in Nepal have long, steep descents, from Larkya La on the Manaslu Circuit to the stone steps of the Annapurna foothills. Here is how to protect your knees.

## Why downhill is harder on the knees

Going up, your muscles push you up the hill. Going down, they lengthen while working to control your body, which is called eccentric work. This kind of effort makes muscles sore, especially if you are not used to it, and it puts a lot of stress around the kneecap. Add a heavy pack and hundreds of steps, and tired knees are no surprise.

## 1. Use two trekking poles

Poles take part of the load off your legs. A 1999 study in the Journal of Sports Sciences found that using two poles on a steep downhill ramp reduced a key knee force by up to about 20% ([NY-NJ Trail Conference summary](https://www.nynjtc.org/node/3489)). Lengthen the poles a little for long descents and plant them slightly ahead of you.

## 2. Slow down and shorten your steps

Big, fast steps down mean hard landings. Take short steps, keep your knees slightly bent, and land softly. On stone steps, go down sideways or zig-zag if the steps are very high. Speed costs knees; pace saves them.

## 3. Train the brakes before you go

The best protection is strong legs that are used to downhill work. Twice a week for at least 6 weeks:

| Exercise | How | Sets x reps |
|---|---|---|
| Step-down | Stand on a step, slowly lower one heel to the floor, step back up | 3 x 8 to 10 each leg |
| Step-up with slow lowering | Step up, then take 3 seconds to step down | 3 x 8 each leg |
| Goblet squat | Slow on the way down | 3 x 10 |
| Reverse lunge | Step back, lower with control | 3 x 8 each leg |
| Wall sit | Back against a wall, knees at 90 degrees | 3 x 30 to 45 sec |

Also walk down stairs during training, not only up. Our [stair climbing workout](../stair-climbing-workout/) and [8-week trek plan](../train-for-nepal-trek-without-hills/) build this in.

## 4. Lighten your pack

Every extra kilo is carried down every step. Leave non-essentials behind, share gear, and if you can, use a porter for the main bag and carry only a day pack with water, snacks and layers.

## 5. Wear shoes with good grip

Slipping and catching yourself is hard on knees. Trekking shoes or boots with good grip, worn in before the trek, help you place your feet with confidence. Trim toenails before long descents to avoid toes hitting the front of the shoe.

## 6. Rest your legs between hard days

After a long descent day, a slower day or a short walk lets your legs recover. If your itinerary allows, avoid two very long descent days in a row.

## 7. Warm up and keep moving

Start descents after a short walk on flatter ground, not straight from sitting cold. At the end of the day, gentle walking and light stretching feel better than lying still.

## When knee pain is a warning

Normal tiredness and muscle soreness ease with rest. Stop and get advice if you have:

- Sharp pain inside the knee, or pain that gets worse with every step
- Swelling, locking, or the knee giving way
- Pain that does not settle after a few days of rest

A physiotherapist can find the cause. If you already have a knee problem, see one before your trek.

## A simple routine for the evening after a long descent

1. Walk slowly for 10 minutes after you arrive instead of sitting straight down.
2. Gently stretch the front and back of your thighs and your calves, 30 seconds each.
3. Raise your legs on your backpack for 10 minutes while you rest.
4. Drink water and eat a proper meal: tired legs recover better with food.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do my knees hurt going downhill but not uphill?
Going downhill, your thigh muscles act as brakes and the load around the kneecap is high, especially with a pack and long descents.

### Do trekking poles really help knees?
Yes. A 1999 study found two poles reduced a key knee force by up to about 20% on a steep downhill.

### What exercises help with knee pain on descents?
Slow step-downs, step-ups with slow lowering, goblet squats, reverse lunges and wall sits, done twice a week for several weeks before the trek.

### Should I wear a knee brace for trekking?
Some people find a light brace or support comfortable, but it does not replace strong legs and good technique. If you have a knee injury, ask a physiotherapist what suits you.

### Is it better to go down stairs sideways?
On very high or uneven steps, going down at an angle or sideways can feel easier on the knees. Keep steps short and controlled.
