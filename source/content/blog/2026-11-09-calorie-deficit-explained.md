A **calorie deficit** simply means **eating a little less energy than your body uses**. Your body then takes the missing energy from stored fat. Every successful fat-loss diet, whether keto, intermittent fasting or "no rice after 6," works because it creates a deficit.

A small, steady deficit beats a big one. For most people, aiming to lose **about 0.5 to 1 kg per week** is sustainable, which is the rate the [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/lose-weight/) describes as healthy.

## How your body uses energy

Every day you burn energy through:

1. **Basic body functions** (breathing, heart, brain): the largest part
2. **Daily movement**: walking, standing, chores
3. **Exercise**: gym, sports
4. **Digesting food**: a small amount

Add these up and you get your daily energy needs. You can estimate yours with our free [calorie calculator](../../calculators/).

## How big should the deficit be?

| Deficit | What happens |
|---|---|
| Very small | Slow progress, easy to stick to |
| **Moderate** (often around 300–600 kcal a day) | **Steady fat loss, manageable hunger** |
| Very large (crash diets) | Fast early loss, hunger, muscle loss, often regain |

For many people, a moderate deficit means eating **noticeably less, not drastically less**.

## How to create a deficit with Nepali food (no counting needed)

You can count calories with an app if you like it, but these habits create a deficit for most people:

1. **Use the plate method:** half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter rice.
2. **Reduce rice by about a third** at each khana and add more dal and tarkari.
3. **Cut sugar in tea** and drop cold drinks and packet juices.
4. **Swap fried snacks** for eggs, fruit, curd or chana.
5. **Cook with less oil and ghee.** A little goes a long way.
6. **Walk more:** 7,000 to 10,000 steps a day.
7. **Eat protein at every meal** so you feel full.

## Why crash diets backfire

- **Muscle loss:** your body breaks down muscle for energy, especially if protein is low.
- **Constant hunger** leads to binge eating.
- **Lower energy** means you move less without noticing.
- **They're impossible to keep up,** so the weight returns.

## Deficit without losing muscle

- **Keep protein high:** about 1.6 g per kg of body weight is a common target when dieting.
- **Strength train 2 to 4 times a week.**
- **Lose slowly.**

## How to know it's working

- Your **weekly average weight** trends down.
- Your **waist measurement** shrinks.
- Clothes feel looser.

If nothing changes for 3 weeks while you're consistent, reduce portions slightly or add more walking. See [why am I not losing weight?](../why-am-i-not-losing-weight/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many calories should I eat to lose weight?

It depends on your size, age, sex and activity. Use a calculator to estimate your needs, then eat a moderate amount less. Adjust based on your weekly results.

### Can I lose fat without counting calories?

Yes. The plate method, less sugar, fewer fried snacks and more walking create a deficit for most people.

### Is a 1,000 calorie deficit safe?

For most people it's too aggressive and hard to sustain without losing muscle. Aim for a moderate deficit unless supervised by a professional.

### Does a calorie deficit slow metabolism?

Your body adapts a little when you eat less for a long time, mostly by moving less. Keeping protein high, lifting weights and staying active helps.
