A good warm-up takes **about 10 minutes** and does three jobs: it **raises your body temperature**, **loosens your joints**, and **rehearses the movements** you're about to do. It is especially important on cold winter mornings and before heavy lifting.

## The 10-minute routine

### Part 1: Light cardio (3 to 5 minutes)

Easy pace on a bike, treadmill, cross-trainer or skipping rope. You should feel warmer and breathe a little faster, but still be able to talk.

### Part 2: Mobility drills (4 minutes)

Do each for about 30 to 40 seconds:

1. **Arm circles**, small to big, forward and back
2. **Band pull-aparts** or arm swings across the chest
3. **Hip circles** and leg swings, front-to-back and side-to-side
4. **Bodyweight squats** with a pause at the bottom
5. **Walking lunges** with a twist
6. **Cat-cow** on hands and knees
7. **Hip hinge** (good mornings with no weight)
8. **Ankle circles** and calf raises

### Part 3: Warm-up sets (2 to 3 minutes per main lift)

Before your first heavy exercise, build up gradually:

| Set | Weight | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty bar or very light | 10 |
| 2 | About 50% of your working weight | 5–8 |
| 3 | About 70–80% | 3 |
| Then | Working sets | As planned |

You don't need warm-up sets for every exercise, mainly the first big lift of the session.

## Static stretching: before or after?

Long static stretches (holding for 30 to 60 seconds) **right before heavy lifting** may slightly reduce strength for that session. Save longer stretches for **after training** or a separate mobility session. Short, gentle stretches as part of a warm-up are fine.

## Warm-ups for specific days

- **Leg day:** add glute bridges, goblet squats with a light weight and walking lunges.
- **Upper body:** add band pull-aparts, push-ups and light dumbbell presses.
- **Cardio or running:** start with brisk walking, then easy jogging, then a few short faster strides.

## Winter warm-ups in Nepal

Cold mornings, especially in Kathmandu and hill areas, make muscles and joints stiff. In winter:

- Make the cardio part **5 to 7 minutes** instead of 3.
- **Keep a light jacket on** until you're warm.
- Add a few extra warm-up sets for heavy lifts.

## Cool-down (5 minutes)

After training, slow walking for 2 to 3 minutes, then gentle stretches for the muscles you trained, holding each for 20 to 30 seconds. It doesn't prevent soreness much, but it feels good and helps you relax.

## Signs your warm-up worked

You don't need a heart-rate monitor to know if you're ready. After a good warm-up:

- You feel **slightly warm**, maybe a light sweat, but not tired.
- Your **joints move freely**: squatting to depth or reaching overhead feels smoother than when you walked in.
- Your **first working set feels like the right weight**, not surprisingly heavy.
- Your breathing is a little faster, but you could hold a conversation.

If you're out of breath or your legs feel heavy before your first real set, you overdid it. Cut the cardio back to 3 minutes.

## Common warm-up mistakes

- **Skipping it when short on time.** Cut your workout by one exercise instead. Cold muscles and joints are when tweaks happen.
- **Long static stretches before heavy lifting.** Holding stretches for a minute or more right before lifting can slightly reduce strength. Save them for after.
- **Warming up the wrong body part.** Ten minutes on the treadmill does little for your shoulders on a bench-press day. Always add mobility and warm-up sets for the joints you'll load.
- **Jumping straight to your working weight.** Ramp up in 2–4 steps, as in Part 3.
- **Turning the warm-up into a workout.** It should prepare you, not drain you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a warm-up really necessary?

It prepares your body for harder work, helps you perform better and may reduce injury risk, especially for heavy or explosive training.

### How long should a warm-up be?

About 5 to 10 minutes for most sessions, a little longer in cold weather or before very heavy lifting.

### Should I stretch before lifting?

Dynamic movements (moving stretches) are best before lifting. Save long static stretches for afterwards.

### Can I use the treadmill as my whole warm-up?

It's a good start, but add mobility drills and warm-up sets for the specific exercises you'll do.

### Should I warm up before cardio too?

Yes. Start your run, cycle or skipping session with 3–5 minutes at an easy pace before you speed up.

### Do I need to warm up for every exercise?

Do warm-up sets for your first exercise of the day and for any new body part. Later exercises for muscles that are already warm usually need only one lighter set.
