A good warm-up before running takes about 10 minutes and has three parts: **easy movement to raise your heart rate, dynamic drills to loosen your hips and legs, and a few short faster runs called strides.** Save long, still stretches for after the run.

![An Apex Fitness Centre member with a headband warming up near the cardio machines](images/apex-member-headband-before-workout.jpg)
*Warming up before a cardio session at Apex Fitness Centre.*

## Why warm up?

Starting a run from cold can make the first kilometre feel heavy. A short warm-up gradually raises your heart rate and breathing, warms your muscles and gets your joints moving, so the run feels smoother from the start. It also gives you a moment to notice any niggle before you speed up.

## The 10-minute routine

### Part 1: Easy movement (4 to 5 minutes)
Brisk walk for 1 to 2 minutes, then jog very slowly for 3 minutes. You should be able to talk easily. On a treadmill, start at walking speed and build up.

### Part 2: Dynamic drills (3 to 4 minutes)
Do each for about 20 to 30 seconds:

| Drill | How |
|---|---|
| Leg swings (front and back) | Hold a wall, swing one leg forward and back, 10 each side |
| Leg swings (side to side) | Swing the leg across your body and out, 10 each side |
| Walking lunges | 5 slow lunges each leg |
| High knees | Light, quick steps lifting your knees |
| Butt kicks | Quick steps bringing your heels towards your bottom |
| Ankle circles | 10 circles each direction, each ankle |

### Part 3: Strides (2 minutes)
Run 3 or 4 times for about 15 to 20 seconds at a quick, relaxed pace, not a sprint, with an easy walk back between them. Then start your run.

## Warm-up for different runs

| Run type | Warm-up |
|---|---|
| Easy run | 5 minutes of walking and very slow jogging; drills are optional |
| Long run | 5 to 10 minutes; start the run itself slower than your normal pace |
| Intervals or a fast 5K | The full 10 minutes, including 3 or 4 strides |
| Hill run | Full warm-up, plus 2 easy hill climbs before the hard ones |
| Early morning | A few extra minutes; you are stiffer after sleep |

The harder and faster the session, the more warm-up you need.

## Common warm-up mistakes

- Skipping it on cold mornings, when your body needs it most.
- Doing long, still stretches first instead of moving.
- Turning the warm-up into a workout: it should leave you ready, not tired.
- Starting the run fast straight after the warm-up. Ease into your pace over the first kilometre.

## Short on time?

Even 5 minutes helps: 3 minutes of slow jogging, then 30 seconds each of leg swings, high knees and butt kicks.

## Static stretching: before or after?

Long, still stretches held for 30 seconds or more are better saved for after your run, when your muscles are warm and you are cooling down. Before a run, moving stretches like the drills above prepare you better.

## The cool-down (5 to 10 minutes)

1. Walk or jog very slowly for 3 to 5 minutes.
2. Then stretch gently, about 30 seconds each:
    - Calves against a wall
    - Front of the thigh (hold your foot behind you)
    - Back of the thigh (one heel on a low step, lean forward)
    - Hip flexors (half-kneeling lunge)
    - Glutes (figure-four stretch on your back)

## Running in heat or at altitude

In hot weather, warm up for a little less time and in the shade; your body is already warm. See [exercising in hot weather](../exercising-in-hot-weather/). At altitude on a trek, start every day slowly for the same reason: your body needs time.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I warm up before running?
About 10 minutes for most runs: 4 to 5 minutes of easy movement, a few minutes of drills, and 3 or 4 strides.

### Should I stretch before running?
Do moving stretches and drills before; save long, still stretches for after the run.

### Do I need to warm up for an easy jog?
A short warm-up still helps. Start the first few minutes of the jog very slowly.

### What are strides in running?
Short runs of 15 to 20 seconds at a quick, relaxed pace, with walking in between, to get your legs ready for running faster.

### Why do my legs feel heavy at the start of a run?
Usually because you started too fast or without a warm-up. Start slower and give your body the first 10 minutes to settle.
