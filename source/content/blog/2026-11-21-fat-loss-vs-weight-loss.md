**Weight loss** means the number on the scale goes down, from any source: fat, muscle, water or food in your gut. **Fat loss** means you're losing body fat specifically. The goal for almost everyone is **fat loss while keeping muscle**, because that's what makes you look leaner, feel stronger and keep the weight off.

## Why the difference matters

Two people can both lose 5 kg:

- **Person A** crash diets with no exercise and little protein: loses fat, but also a lot of muscle and water. Looks smaller but "soft," feels weak, and regains quickly.
- **Person B** eats a moderate deficit with plenty of protein and lifts weights: loses mostly fat, keeps muscle. Looks noticeably leaner and keeps the results.

Same number on the scale, very different outcomes.

## What makes the scale go up and down

| Factor | Effect on the scale |
|---|---|
| Body fat | Slow, steady change |
| Muscle | Slow change |
| Water | Can change by 1–2 kg in a day |
| Salt and carbs | More water held after salty or carb-heavy meals |
| Food in your gut | Changes daily |
| Menstrual cycle | Water changes across the month |

That's why daily weighing can be confusing.

## Better ways to measure progress

1. **Weekly average weight:** weigh 3 to 7 mornings a week and average them.
2. **Waist measurement** every 2 weeks at the belly button. See [how to lose belly fat](../how-to-lose-belly-fat/).
3. **Monthly photos:** front, side and back, same light, same clothes.
4. **Strength in the gym:** if your lifts are holding or rising during a diet, you're likely keeping muscle.
5. **How clothes fit.**

## How to lose fat, not muscle

- **Moderate calorie deficit:** see [calorie deficit explained](../calorie-deficit-explained/).
- **High protein:** see [how much protein you need](../how-much-protein-do-you-need/).
- **Strength training 2 to 4 times a week:** see [cardio vs weights for fat loss](../cardio-vs-weights-for-fat-loss/).
- **Daily walking.**
- **Sleep 7 to 9 hours.**
- **Be patient:** about 0.5 to 1 kg a week is a sensible pace for most people.

## "I'm losing inches but not weight"

That's often good news. It usually means you're losing fat while gaining a little muscle or holding some water, especially when you're new to strength training. Keep going.

## BMI is a rough guide

BMI uses only height and weight. It doesn't tell fat from muscle. A muscular person can have a "high" BMI while being lean. Waist size and how you look and feel tell you more. You can still check yours with our free [BMI calculator](../../calculators/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I lose fat without losing weight?

Yes. If you build muscle while losing fat (common for beginners), your weight may stay similar while your body looks leaner.

### How fast can you lose fat?

A steady pace of about 0.5 to 1 kg a week is realistic for most people. Faster loss often includes more water and muscle.

### Does muscle weigh more than fat?

A kilo is a kilo, but muscle is denser than fat, so it takes up less space. That's why you can look leaner at the same weight.

### Why do I gain weight after a heavy meal?

Mostly food and water, not fat. It usually goes back down in a day or two.
