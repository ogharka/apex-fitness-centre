A good rule is that **a loaded backpack for a multi-day trek should weigh no more than about 20% of your body weight, and a day pack no more than about 10%.** That is the guideline from outdoor retailer REI ([REI](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/backpacking-weight.html)). On teahouse treks in Nepal, where you sleep and eat in lodges, you can usually carry much less.

## How much is that for you?

| Your body weight | Day pack (10%) | Full trek pack (20%) |
|---|---|---|
| 50 kg | 5 kg | 10 kg |
| 60 kg | 6 kg | 12 kg |
| 70 kg | 7 kg | 14 kg |
| 80 kg | 8 kg | 16 kg |

These are upper limits, not targets. Lighter is easier on your legs, back and knees.

## With a porter or without?

Most trekkers in Nepal choose one of two set-ups:

- **With a porter:** you carry a day pack (water, snacks, layers, rain jacket, phone, documents) and the porter carries your main bag. Your pack is usually around 5 to 7 kg. Agencies often set a weight limit for the porter's bag; ask yours.
- Without a porter: you carry everything. On a teahouse trek you do not need a tent, stove or food for many days, so a well-packed bag can stay well under 20% of body weight.

Using a porter also supports local jobs and lets you enjoy the trek more, especially on long or high routes.

## What to pack (teahouse trek)

**Carry every day:**
- Water bottle or bladder (about 1.5 to 2 litres)
- Snacks for between teahouses
- Warm layer and rain jacket
- Hat, gloves, sunglasses, sunscreen
- Headlamp
- Basic first aid and your medicines
- Phone, money, permits and ID

In the main bag:
- 2 or 3 sets of trekking clothes
- Thermal layers and a down jacket for evenings
- Sleeping bag (teahouse blankets may not be enough higher up)
- Wash kit and quick-dry towel
- Sandals or light shoes for evenings

## What to leave behind

- More than 2 or 3 changes of clothes: you can wash and re-wear.
- Heavy jeans and cotton clothes: they are heavy and dry slowly.
- Full-size toiletries: decant into small bottles.
- Books and extra electronics: one phone and a power bank are enough.
- Lots of food from home: teahouses serve meals, and snacks are sold on the trail.

## Pack it right

- **Heavy items close to your back** and in the middle of the bag.
- Tighten the hip belt so most of the weight sits on your hips, not your shoulders.
- Adjust the shoulder straps so they are snug but not doing all the work.
- Use small bags inside to keep things organised and dry.

## Weigh your pack before you go

Guessing is easy to get wrong. Two simple ways to weigh your bag at home:

1. **Bathroom scale:** weigh yourself, then weigh yourself holding the packed bag. The difference is the bag.
2. Luggage scale: hang the bag from a cheap hand-held luggage scale.

Do this with water bottles full and snacks packed, because that is how you will carry it. If the number is above your limit, take everything out, make two piles, "need" and "nice to have", and leave the second pile at your hotel in Kathmandu or Pokhara.

## Weight changes during the trek

Your pack is not the same weight every day:

- **Water** is the heaviest thing you carry. Carry what you need to the next refill point, not more.
- Layers move from your body to your bag as the day warms up, so leave space for them.
- Higher up, you may carry more warm clothing, while every step feels harder. Plan your pack for the hardest days, not the first one.

## Train with your pack

The pack you train with should match the pack you trek with. Start light and add a kilo every week or two. ## Your knees will thank you

Every extra kilo is carried down every step. If your knees hurt on descents, a lighter pack is one of the easiest fixes. See [7 ways to stop knee pain when hiking downhill](../knee-pain-hiking-downhill/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How heavy should my backpack be for Everest Base Camp?
With a porter, usually a day pack of around 5 to 7 kg. Carrying everything yourself, aim to stay under about 20% of your body weight, and ideally well below it.

### What is the 20% rule for backpacking?
A guideline that a loaded multi-day pack should weigh no more than about 20% of your body weight. For day hikes, the guideline is about 10%.

### Do I need a porter in Nepal?
No, but many trekkers use one to carry the main bag. It makes long and high treks easier and supports local workers.

### How much water should I carry on a trek?
Most trekkers carry about 1.5 to 2 litres and refill at teahouses, treating or filtering the water.

### Should I train with a heavy backpack?
Train with the weight you will carry, building up slowly over several weeks.
