What is a one-rep max?

Your one-rep max (1RM) is the heaviest weight you can lift once with good technique. Coaches use it to set training weights as a percentage, for example 3 sets of 5 at 80% of 1RM.

Testing a true 1RM is tiring and carries more risk, especially for beginners. Estimating it from a harder set of several reps is safer and close enough for planning.

How the calculator works

We use two of the most common formulas and show the average:

  • Epley: 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30)
  • Brzycki: 1RM = weight × 36 ÷ (37 − reps)

Both are most accurate when the set is close to failure and between about 2 and 10 reps. With high reps the estimates spread out, because muscular endurance varies a lot from person to person.

Recalculate every 4–6 weeks using a recent hard set, so your training weights keep up with your progress.

Safety tips

  • Warm up with lighter sets first.
  • Use safety bars or a spotter for bench press and squats.
  • Stop a set when your form breaks down.

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is a one rep max calculator?

For sets of about 2 to 10 reps taken close to failure, estimates are usually within a few kilograms of a tested max. They are less accurate for very high-rep sets.

Which formula is best for 1RM?

Epley and Brzycki give similar results at low reps. We average them. No formula is perfect for everyone.

Should beginners test their 1RM?

Usually not. Beginners improve quickly and benefit more from learning technique. An estimate from a set of 5 to 8 reps is safer.

Does this work for deadlift and squat?

Yes. It works for most big barbell and dumbbell lifts, as long as the set was hard and done with good form.

What percentage of my max should I train at?

Muscle growth usually works well at 60 to 85% of your max for 6 to 15 reps. Strength training often uses 80 to 95% for 1 to 6 reps.
