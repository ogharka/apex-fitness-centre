What is an ideal weight?

There is no single perfect weight for any height. Instead, health bodies use a healthy weight range, based on a body mass index (BMI) of 18.5 to 24.9 for adults. Within that range, the right weight for you depends on your muscle, frame size, age and health.

South Asian cut-offs

People of South Asian background, including Nepali, Indian and Bangladeshi people, tend to develop type 2 diabetes and heart disease at a lower BMI. That is why the World Health Organization expert consultation and some national guidelines use lower action points, such as a BMI of 23. The calculator shows this range too.

The classic formulas

Older formulas such as Devine (1974), Robinson (1983), Miller (1983) and Hamwi (1964) were developed mainly to calculate medicine doses. They give a single number from height and sex, and differ from each other by several kilograms. We show them for comparison, but the BMI range is a better guide.

Weight is only part of the story

  • Waist size: fat around the middle carries more health risk. Many guidelines suggest keeping your waist under half your height.
  • Muscle: people who lift weights can be heavier than the formulas suggest and still be lean and healthy. Our body fat calculator gives a better picture.
  • Habits matter more than the number: activity, sleep, food quality and strength all improve health at any weight.

Frequently asked questions

What is the ideal weight for my height?

For adults, a healthy weight is usually the range that gives a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9. For example, at 170 cm that is about 53 to 72 kg. For South Asian adults, staying under a BMI of 23 (about 66 kg at 170 cm) is often advised.

Which ideal weight formula is most accurate?

None is precise for everyone. The formulas were designed for medicine dosing and give single numbers. The BMI-based healthy range is more useful for most people.

Can I be healthy above my ideal weight?

Yes, especially if you carry a lot of muscle. Waist size, fitness, blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol tell you more than weight alone.

Is the ideal weight different for women?

The BMI range is the same for adult men and women, but the classic formulas give women slightly lower numbers because they were based on average body size differences.

Does this work for teenagers?

No. Children and teenagers are still growing and should be assessed with growth charts for their age and sex.
