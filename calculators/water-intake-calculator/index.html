How much water do you need?

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) sets adequate total water intakes of about 2.0 litres a day for women and 2.5 litres a day for men. That includes water from food, which usually provides around 20–30% of the total. The rest comes from drinks: water, tea, milk and other beverages all count.

How the calculator works

  • Baseline from drinks: about 80% of the EFSA total (1.6 L for women, 2.0 L for men).
  • Exercise: about 0.6 L extra per hour. Sweat losses vary widely, often 0.5–1.5 L an hour or more in the heat.
  • Hot weather: about 0.5 L extra.

These are averages. Your needs depend on body size, sweat rate, diet and health.

Simple ways to stay hydrated

  • Check your urine: pale yellow usually means you are drinking enough.
  • Drink with every meal and keep a bottle with you.
  • Before training: a glass of water 1–2 hours before.
  • During long or hot sessions: sip regularly, and consider electrolytes if you sweat heavily for over an hour.

Can you drink too much?

Yes. Drinking very large amounts quickly, especially during long endurance events, can dilute blood sodium (hyponatraemia), which is dangerous. Drink to thirst and use the guide above rather than forcing litres. People with heart or kidney conditions should follow their doctor's fluid advice.

Frequently asked questions

How much water should I drink a day?

Most adults need about 2 to 2.5 litres of total water a day from food and drinks, which usually means around 1.6 to 2 litres from drinks, plus more when exercising or in hot weather.

Does tea or coffee count towards water intake?

Yes. Tea, coffee and milk all count. Caffeine has a mild effect on urine, but moderate amounts still hydrate you overall.

How much water should I drink when working out?

Drink a glass before training and sip during it. Around 0.5 to 1 litre per hour of exercise is common, more in hot weather.

Is 3 litres of water a day too much?

For many active people, especially in hot weather, 3 litres is fine. Problems usually only happen with very large amounts in a short time.

Do I need more water to build muscle?

Not specifically, but active people and those eating more protein usually feel better drinking a little more through the day.
