How the calculator works

We use MET values from the 2024 Adult Compendium of Physical Activities, the standard list of how much energy different activities use. One MET is roughly 1 kcal per kg of body weight per hour, so:

Calories burned = MET × your weight in kg × hours walking

The result is a rough estimate. Real trek days mix steep climbs, flat sections and long descents, and cold weather and altitude change how much energy you use.

What to do with the number

  • Eat more than you think. Long trek days can use far more energy than a normal day, and appetite often drops at altitude. Eat at every meal even if you are not hungry.
  • Snack every 1 to 1.5 hours while walking: nuts, dried fruit, biscuits, chocolate or boiled eggs from the teahouse.
  • Dal bhat is a good trek fuel: rice for energy, dal for protein, and it is easy to digest. See how much protein is in dal bhat.
  • Drink regularly. Use the water intake calculator as a starting point and add more on long, hot or high days.

Training for the trek

Being fit makes long days easier, but it does not protect you from altitude sickness: a slow ascent does. For training plans, start with our trekking fitness guides.

Frequently asked questions

How many calories do you burn trekking in Nepal?

It depends on your weight, the hours you walk, the steepness and your pack. For example, a 70 kg person walking 6 hours on a hilly trail with a day pack burns roughly 3,300 kcal on that walking alone (7.8 MET x 70 kg x 6 hours), on top of the energy used the rest of the day.

Do you burn more calories at high altitude?

Your body can use more energy at altitude, and cold adds to it, while appetite often drops. That is why many trekkers lose weight on long treks. This calculator does not add an altitude factor, so treat high-altitude days as at least this much.

Does carrying a backpack burn more calories?

Yes. A heavier load on steep ground uses noticeably more energy, which is why the full-pack options use higher MET values.

Is walking downhill easier on calories?

Downhill walking uses less energy than climbing, but it is hard on the legs and knees. The day types here are averages that include both.

Should I try to lose weight on a trek?

No. Eat enough to keep your energy up and stay safe. Running low on food makes you tired, cold and more likely to slip or make poor decisions.
