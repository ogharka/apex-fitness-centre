How much protein do you need?

The basic recommendation for healthy adults is 0.8 g of protein per kg of body weight a day. That is the minimum to avoid deficiency, not the best amount for people who train. Research on people doing strength training points to higher intakes:

GoalProtein per kg per dayExample for 70 kg
Little exercise0.8 g56 g
Regular exercise or sport1.2–1.6 g84–112 g
Building muscle1.6–2.2 g112–154 g
Losing fat while lifting1.8–2.2 g126–154 g
Adults over 651.0–1.2 g (more if training)70–84 g

A large meta-analysis found that muscle gains from strength training levelled off at around 1.6 g per kg a day, with an upper confidence limit of about 2.2 g. The International Society of Sports Nutrition recommends 1.4–2.0 g per kg for most exercising people.

How to use your result

  • Spread it out. Three or four meals with 20–40 g of protein each works well for most people.
  • Food first. Eggs, chicken, fish, meat, dal, chana, rajma, paneer, curd, milk, tofu and soybeans all count.
  • Use a shake only to fill the gap. Whey or plant protein is convenient, not essential.
  • Overweight? Use a goal weight, or your weight minus some of the extra fat, rather than your current weight, or the target will be higher than you need.

Is too much protein harmful?

For healthy adults, intakes in these ranges are considered safe. People with kidney disease, or at high risk of it, should follow their doctor's advice, because they may need to limit protein.

Frequently asked questions

How much protein do I need to build muscle?

Most research suggests about 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight a day, and up to 2.2 g for some people. For a 70 kg person that is about 112–154 g a day, alongside regular strength training.

Is 100 g of protein a day enough?

It depends on your weight and goal. For a 60–65 kg person building muscle, 100 g is close to the target. A heavier person or someone dieting while lifting may need more.

Should I use my current weight or goal weight?

Use your current weight if you are fairly lean. If you are carrying a lot of extra fat, use a goal weight so the target is realistic.

Can vegetarians get enough protein?

Yes. Combine dal, chana, rajma, soybeans, paneer, curd, milk and tofu across the day, and add a protein powder if needed.

Can I eat all my protein in one meal?

You can, but spreading it across three or four meals is easier to digest and may be slightly better for building muscle.

Sources

  • ISSN position stand: protein and exercise (Jäger et al., 2017)
